# MLB Model Audit Findings - 2026-07-26

## Data Reviewed

Five workbooks pooled and deduplicated by (date, player, pitcher, rank):

- `audit/MLB Picks Analysis 6.17.26.xlsx`, `6.27.26`, `7.3.26`, `7.18.26`, `7.26.26`
- 8,556 full-slate audit rows per model across **38 slates**, June 2 - July 25, 2026
- 468 saved hit picks / 391 saved HR picks, of which 99 hit / 70 HR carry the
  current-versus-classic metadata added on July 17

Implementations reviewed: `index.html` (hit), `hr.html` (HR).

## Executive Conclusion

The perceived decline is two different problems, and neither is the scoring math.

1. **Hit ranking never degraded.** The audit top 10 held at 72.4% / 75.6% / 75.0%
   across June, July 1-15, and July 16-25. What degraded is the *displayed list*:
   saved picks fell 68.1% -> 69.4% -> 63.7%. The union-of-two-top-10s display is
   diluting a ranking that still works.
2. **The HR drop is most likely variance.** Top-10 lift went 1.70x -> 1.81x ->
   1.12x, but the last window is 8 slates / 80 rows (z = -1.6, p ~ 0.11). Every
   re-ranking ablation tried failed to recover it. Do not touch the HR weights.

Separately, both models have a real, large-sample **calibration** error that
matters only for betting against odds: hit probabilities run 2.5 pp too high,
HR probabilities run 2.6 pp too low. Both trace to hardcoded league constants in a
season that is drifting.

The single cleanest defect found is Finding 7: the classic hit model never calls
`calibrateProbability()`, so it displays ~90% on picks that hit 55%.

> This document was revised after independent review. **Finding 3's original
> recommendation to lower `LEAGUE.hPerPa` to 0.207 was wrong and has been
> retracted** - the estimate was Jensen-biased. Finding 2 was re-tested by the
> same method and confirmed. Saved-pick sample sizes throughout were corrected
> downward for cross-workbook duplicates. Findings 6 and 7 were added.

## Finding 1 - The hit display list is the actual regression

Audit rows (pure current model, no display logic) versus saved picks:

| Window | Slates | Audit base | Audit top 10 | Saved picks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| June | 21 | 62.7% | 72.4% | 67.9% (n=162) |
| July 1-15 | 9 | 61.9% | 75.6% | 69.4% (n=98) |
| July 16-25 | 8 | 61.0% | 75.0% | 64.7% (n=99) |

> **Corrected 2026-07-26 after review.** The saved-pick counts originally reported
> here (248 / 196 / 124) double-counted picks that appear in more than one
> workbook. Rates are essentially unchanged and the trend survives.
>
> The cause is a defect in `audit/audit_stats.py`: `dedupe_prediction_rows()`
> keys on `Prediction ID`, but re-exported copies of the same pick carry
> *different* IDs. Across all five workbooks it removed **0** of the 209
> duplicate rows (568 raw -> 568 "unique"). Deduplicating on
> `(date, player, rank)` yields the true 359. Any cross-workbook pooling done
> with that helper is currently overcounting.

The ranking is stable. The list is not. Within the 8 slates that carry model
metadata (n=99 picks):

| Selection rule | n | Hit rate | Picks/slate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **All (shipped today)** | 99 | **64.6%** | 12.4 |
| Consensus AND current top 10 | 40 | **77.5%** | 5.0 |
| Consensus only (both top lists) | 41 | 75.6% | 5.1 |
| Consensus OR current top 5 | 51 | 72.5% | 6.4 |
| Displayed rank 1-8 | 60 | 71.7% | 7.5 |
| Current top 10 | 72 | 69.4% | 9.0 |
| Current-only | 29 | 58.6% | 3.6 |
| Classic-only | 29 | 55.2% | 3.6 |
| Displayed rank 9+ | 39 | 53.8% | 4.9 |

Rank agreement says the same thing continuously: `|currentRank - classicRank| <= 5`
produced 70.2% (n=47) versus 59.6% (n=52) when the models disagreed by more.

Mechanism: `mergedModelPicks()` in both pages unions two independent top-10s
(each capped at 2 per game/team), so 10-20 picks are displayed. `compareHitDisplayPicks`
sorts consensus first, so the tail of the list is by construction the picks only
one model liked - and those hit at 55-59%, below the 61% slate baseline.

Prediction is not the constraint here. Displayed probability barely separates the
groups: consensus predicted 77.3% and delivered 75.6%; non-consensus predicted
74.5% and delivered 56.9%. Agreement carries information the probability does not.

**Caveats.** Consensus 31/41 versus non-consensus 33/58 gives z = 1.92, p ~ 0.055 -
suggestive, not established. The "displayed rank 1-8" and "consensus" cuts overlap
heavily (consensus sorts first), so they are not independent confirmations. And the
HR data shows the *opposite* sign (below), so model agreement is not a general law.
What is robust across every cut is the weaker claim: **the bottom of the union list
is worse than the slate baseline, so showing fewer picks is better.**

## Finding 2 - HR probabilities are systematically ~20-25% too low

8,556 audit rows, 38 slates. Every bucket understates, monotonically:

| Displayed | n | Shipped pred | Actual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.06-0.08 | 1,450 | 6.6% | 8.6% |
| 0.08-0.10 | 2,186 | 8.5% | 11.3% |
| 0.10-0.12 | 2,068 | 10.4% | 13.9% |
| 0.12-0.14 | 1,329 | 12.4% | 14.2% |
| 0.14-0.16 | 885 | 14.4% | 16.7% |
| 0.16-0.18 | 387 | 16.4% | 19.1% |
| 0.18+ | 151 | 18.4% | 22.5% |

Pooled: shipped mean 10.4% versus actual 13.0%.

Root cause: `LEAGUE.hrPerPa = 0.028` is the *all-batters* rate, but the model only
ever scores *starting-lineup* batters. Counting directly - **1,195 home runs in
34,991 estimated PA = 3.42% per PA** - that constant is 22% too low for the
population it is applied to. It is used twice, as the shrink target for
`seasonHrRate` (`batterPaPrior: 220`) and as the anchor inside
`calibrateProbability`, so one low value compounds.

> **Confirmed on review.** The Jensen-bias objection that correctly overturned
> Finding 3 does *not* transfer here. `1 - (1-p)^n` is nearly linear at
> home-run-sized probabilities, so the two estimation methods agree: inverting the
> game rate gives 0.0336 and direct counting gives 0.0342. Jensen bias is severe
> for common events and negligible for rare ones. This finding survives the
> stronger method - and the direct count is *further* from 0.028, not closer.

A constant sweep confirms the anchor alone cannot close the gap: raising
`LEAGUE.hrPerPa` to 0.036 only moves bias from -2.6 pp to -1.6 pp while distorting
the low end. The error is multiplicative (actual / shipped ~ 1.2-1.25 across the
whole range).

**But a static uplift is the wrong fix,** because the bias is not stable:

| Window | Shipped | Actual | Bias |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June | 10.37% | 13.48% | -3.11 pp |
| July 1-15 | 10.35% | 13.50% | -3.15 pp |
| July 16-25 | 10.48% | 11.18% | **-0.70 pp** |

The recent window looks well calibrated, but note *why*: shipped probability barely
moved (10.37 -> 10.35 -> 10.48) because the constant is hardcoded. The environment
fell to meet a static prediction. That is coincidence, not accuracy - if HR rates
revert toward 13.5%, the -3 pp bias returns immediately. Applying a fixed +25%
uplift now would overshoot badly in the current environment.

The same static-constant problem explains Finding 3's *growing* hit bias, in the
opposite direction: hit rates drifted down (62.73% -> 61.05%) while shipped stayed
near 64.6%.

Suggested change:

- Replace both hardcoded league anchors with a **trailing-window estimate** of
  league rate for the scored population (starting lineups), recomputed per slate.
  This fixes HR underconfidence, hit overconfidence, and the drift in both.
- Keep the shrink target separate from the calibration anchor rather than reusing
  one constant for both.
- Do not apply a fixed multiplier to either model.

Note that changing `LEAGUE.hrPerPa` is **not** purely cosmetic: it is also the
shrink target, so it changes per-batter estimates and can reorder players. If the
goal is only a trustworthy displayed number, prefer splitting the field into a
`rankProb` (untouched, used for ordering) and an empirically calibrated
`displayProb` (used for odds comparison), and track them separately.

## Finding 3 - Hit probabilities are 2.5 pp too high (opposite direction)

Same 8,556 rows:

| Displayed | n | Shipped pred | Actual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.50-0.55 | 289 | 52.9% | 56.1% |
| 0.55-0.60 | 1,886 | 58.0% | 57.7% |
| 0.60-0.65 | 2,109 | 62.5% | 58.5% |
| 0.65-0.70 | 2,782 | 67.3% | 64.8% |
| 0.70-0.75 | 1,093 | 72.2% | 68.1% |
| 0.75+ | 353 | 76.2% | 72.5% |

Overstated by 2.5-4.1 pp in exactly the 0.60-0.75 band the picks come from. Saved
picks show it larger still: predicted 75.7%, actual 64.6%.

The bias is also **growing**, which the pooled figure hides:

| Window | Shipped | Actual | Bias |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June | 64.38% | 62.73% | +1.65 pp |
| July 1-15 | 64.87% | 61.89% | +2.98 pp |
| July 16-25 | 64.64% | 61.05% | +3.59 pp |

> **Corrected 2026-07-26 after review.** This section originally blamed
> `LEAGUE.hPerPa = 0.223` and recommended lowering it to 0.207. **That was wrong -
> do not make that change.** The 0.207 figure came from inverting the game-level
> "at least one hit" rate through the mean expected PA. Because
> `1 - (1-p)^n` is strongly concave at hit-sized probabilities, that inversion is
> Jensen-biased downward across a heterogeneous population.
>
> Counting directly instead: **7,752 hits in 30,965 at-bats = .2503 BA, which is
> 22.16% per PA** at an AB/PA ratio of 0.885. `LEAGUE.hPerPa = 0.223` is
> essentially correct and should be left alone.

The anchor is therefore exonerated, and so is the independence assumption: feeding
the directly-measured p = 0.2216 through actual per-game PAs predicts 62.85%
against an actual 62.16% - a gap of only +0.70 pp, i.e. an effective-PA multiplier
of 0.980. Within-game PA correlation is real but small.

That leaves the multiplicative adjustment stack
(`opsAdj * recentAdj * contactAdj * pitcherWeakness`) as the residual source of the
remaining ~2 pp, since it nets above 1.0 across the population. There is no clean
single constant to fix. The recentAdj change in Finding 6 addresses part of it
directly - hot picks are the most overstated group.

**Also ruled out:** the expected-PA table. Measured against actual at-bats,
`expectedPAs` is accurate - overall ratio 1.005. Only leadoff is meaningfully
generous (4.70 modeled versus 4.53 estimated, ratio 0.963).

## Finding 4 - Do not change the HR scoring weights

| Window | Slates | Base | Top 10 | Top 20 | Top-10 lift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| June | 21 | 13.5% | 22.9% | 20.5% | 1.70x |
| July 1-15 | 9 | 13.5% | 24.4% | 24.4% | 1.81x |
| July 16-25 | 8 | 11.2% | 12.5% | 15.6% | 1.12x |

The last window is 8 slates / 80 top-10 rows. Against the prior 1.75x lift on an
11.2% base, expected ~15.7 home runs and observed 10: z = -1.6, p ~ 0.11 - and rows
cluster 10 per slate, so the effective sample is smaller and the true p larger.
Top 20 is weaker still (z = -1.28).

The league environment also moved: base fell 13.5% -> 11.2%, with 9.3% on July 24
and 5.4% on July 25.

Critically, the factor edges did **not** decay. Top-versus-bottom quartile spread,
early versus late half of the 7.26 workbook:

| Factor | Early edge | Late edge |
|---|---:|---:|
| HR Chance | +6.1 pp | +6.7 pp |
| Single-trip HR rate | +6.3 pp | +7.6 pp |
| Power adj | +6.7 pp | +5.7 pp |
| TTO adj | +5.0 pp | +5.2 pp |
| Pitcher risk | +0.6 pp | +3.4 pp |
| Park | +0.8 pp | +0.5 pp |

And no re-ranking recovers the late window (top 5 / 10 / 20, late):

| Variant | Late | All 16 slates |
|---|---:|---:|
| Baseline (shipped) | 12.5 / 12.5 / 15.6 | 20.0 / 19.4 / 20.9 |
| Drop park | 10.0 / 15.0 / 16.2 | 20.0 / 23.1 / 20.3 |
| Drop pitcher risk | 12.5 / 17.5 / 14.4 | 21.2 / 23.1 / 19.1 |
| Drop park + pitcher risk | 12.5 / 15.0 / 14.4 | 22.5 / 21.9 / 19.7 |
| Damp power (isoAdj^0.5) | 10.0 / 11.2 / 15.6 | 18.8 / 20.0 / 20.3 |
| Boost power (isoAdj^1.5) | 12.5 / 13.8 / 14.4 | 18.8 / 20.6 / 20.0 |
| Lineup slots 1-5 only | 12.5 / 12.5 / 16.2 | 20.0 / 19.4 / 21.2 |

Everything lands at 12-16% late. There is no tweak this data supports.

Worth noting for later: pitcher risk and park earn almost nothing (+0.6/+3.4 pp and
+0.8/+0.5 pp), and dropping both did not hurt the pooled result (top 10 21.9%
versus 19.4% baseline). They are close to dead weight, but harmless, and the sample
does not justify removing them.

The HR consensus signal runs *opposite* to the hit signal - consensus 13.3% (n=30),
current-only 20.0% (n=20), classic-only 15.0% (n=20); displayed rank 1-8 at 10.0%
versus 9+ at 23.3%. At these sample sizes this is noise, but it is the reason
Finding 1 should not be generalized into "model agreement is always informative."

## Finding 5 - Structural fragility in the HR model (hypothesis, not measured)

`score()` in `hr.html` has most of its terms disabled:

- `PLATOON` is all `1.00` (lines 942-946 define real values only for *classic*)
- `contactAdj = 1.0`, `recentAdj = 1.0`, `awayAdj = 1.0` (hardcoded, lines 903-905)
- `hrGameEnvironmentFactors()` returns `1.0` for every game (lines 865-871)

The live HR model is therefore `seasonHrRate * isoAdj * pitcherRisk * parkAdj` -
four terms, all season-long. Nothing in it responds to recent form.

The consequence is visible in the exports. Over the 8 late slates, the top 20 held
only 54 distinct players across 160 slots - the same leaderboard re-served daily:

| Player | Late appearances | HR |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ben Rice | 8 | 1 |
| Junior Caminero | 8 | 1 |
| James Wood | 7 | 0 |
| Munetaka Murakami | 7 | 0 |
| Matt Olson | 7 | 2 |
| Yordan Alvarez | 7 | 2 |
| Shohei Ohtani | 6 | 0 |
| Kyle Schwarber | 6 | 1 |

James Wood went 6-for-7 in the early half and 0-for-7 in the late half with no
change in how the model rated him. When a handful of names carry the list and they
go cold together, the whole list fails - which is one plausible reading of Finding 4
that variance alone does not explain.

`classicScore()` already implements both `recentAdj` (30% weight, 35-PA prior) and
`contactAdj`, which makes it the natural comparison.

**This cannot be tested from the current exports** - `Recent Adj` is `1.000` in
every audit row and every saved HR pick, so there is no variation to regress
against. It needs a forward test, not a backtest.

## Finding 6 - The hit recent-form adjustment is net negative

Added after review. `recentAdj` is hardcoded to 1.0 in the audit backtest, so it is
invisible in full-slate rows - but it varies in saved picks. Across all 37 slates
with saved hit picks (n=359 unique):

| `Recent Adj` | n | Hit rate | Predicted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| cold <0.99 | 26 | 92.3% | 74.2% |
| neutral 0.99-1.01 | 166 | 68.7% | 73.7% |
| hot >1.01 | 167 | **62.3%** | 75.9% |

Monotone in the finer buckets as well: 1.00 -> 69.6%, 1.01 -> 63.0%, 1.02 -> 57.1%,
1.04 -> 50.0%. Predicted probability *rises* with the adjustment while realized
accuracy falls - the model is paying for hot streaks and getting less.

Hot versus everything else is z = -1.94. That leans partly on the small 24/26 cold
bucket; hot versus neutral alone is z = -1.23. Directionally consistent and
monotone, but not established.

**The likely mechanism is a second unused-constant defect.** `MODEL.recentAbPrior: 45`
is declared at `index.html:615` and **never referenced again**. `recentPa` is
fetched and assigned at `index.html:2341` but never read. So `score()` applies
`batter.recentHPerPa` completely **unshrunk**:

```js
const recentAdj = batter.recentHPerPa != null
  ? clamp(1 + 0.15 * (batter.recentHPerPa - seasonHPerPa) / Math.max(seasonHPerPa, 0.05), 0.90, 1.10)
  : 1.0;
```

A batter who went 6-for-12 over the trailing 21 days receives the same full
adjustment as one who went 30-for-60. Every other recent-form term in the codebase
shrinks by sample size - `hr.html:970` uses
`shrink(batter.recentHrPerPa, seasonHrRate, batter.recentPa || 0, 35)`. The hit
model has the sample size on hand and ignores it.

This means **weight and shrinkage are two separate fixes and must not be
confounded.** If the defect is the unshrunk sample, `0.15` may be fine once
shrinkage is applied. Shadow-test four arms, not three:

| Arm | Weight | Shrinkage |
|---|---|---|
| A (current) | 0.15 | none |
| B | 0.05 | none |
| C | 0.15 | `shrink(recentHPerPa, seasonHPerPa, recentPa, 45)` |
| D | 0.00 | n/a |

Export all four ranks for 8-10 slates before switching. Arm C is the one that tests
the actual defect; B alone would treat the symptom and could discard a real signal.

## Finding 7 - The classic hit model never calibrates its probability

Added after review. This is a defect, not a tuning question.
`classicScore()` in `index.html` ends with:

```js
const prob = 1 - Math.pow(1 - pHit, ePAs);
```

It never calls `calibrateProbability()`, unlike `score()`. The classic hit
probabilities are therefore raw and structurally inflated. Observed in the exports:

| Group | n | Classic pred | Actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| both | 41 | 91.0% | 75.6% |
| current-only | 29 | 83.3% | 58.6% |
| classic-only | 29 | 90.2% | 55.2% |

`classicScore()` in `hr.html` **does** calibrate (`leagueProb + (rawProb -
leagueProb) * 0.72`, clamped 0.025-0.28).

> **Two corrections after review.**
>
> **1. Adding the missing call would not fix the selections, and would not even fix
> the number.** Reconstructing each classic pick's `pHit` from `Classic Chance` and
> its lineup slot, then re-applying the calibrated form, changes only **2.1% of
> pairwise orderings** (13 of 619) and alters the top-half set in **1 of 8 slates**.
> Classic-only picks go from a displayed 90.2% to a calibrated **84.9%**, against an
> actual **55.2%**. Calibration is very nearly a monotone rescale here, so it fixes
> neither *who* is chosen nor, adequately, *what number is shown*.
>
> The classic hit model's problem is upstream of calibration. `classicScore()`
> multiplies far looser adjustments than `score()` - `opsAdj` clamped 0.85-1.18
> versus 0.92-1.14, `recentAdj` 0.65-1.45 versus 0.90-1.10, `pitcherWeakness`
> 0.40-2.20 versus 0.78-1.25 - with no damping anywhere. The raw `pHit` is simply
> too high. **Suppress the classic hit percentage rather than calibrating it.**
>
> **2. The claimed link to the reversed HR consensus result is retracted.**
> Consensus is defined by top-10 *rank overlap*, and calibration barely changes
> rank (2.1% of pairs). It therefore cannot explain the hit-versus-HR asymmetry.
> Worse, there was no asymmetry to explain: as noted in Finding 4, the HR consensus
> comparison is 4/20 versus 3/20 - a single home run, z = 0.42. This document
> criticised exactly that over-reading and then built a mechanism to explain it.

The case for demoting classic-only hit picks rests on their observed 55.2%, not on
the calibration defect. The two are separate issues and should be treated as such.

## Recommended Changes, in priority order

Ship now (mechanical or observation-backed, no new modelling):

1. **Make consensus / current-top-10 the primary hit list** (~5/slate, 77.5%
   observed). Move the remainder to a watchlist. Keep exporting dropped picks so
   the counterfactual stays measurable.
2. **Suppress the classic hit percentage** (Finding 7). Do not display 90% - or a
   calibrated 85% - on picks that hit 55%. Calibrating it is insufficient; hiding
   it is correct. This is independent of item 1, which stands on observed results.
3. **Fix `dedupe_prediction_rows()`** in `audit/audit_stats.py` to key on
   `(date, player, rank)`. It currently removes zero cross-workbook duplicates.

Instrument next (needed before any further tuning can be judged):

4. **Split `rankProb` from `displayProb`** (Findings 2 and 3). Leave the scoring
   probability untouched for ordering; add a separately tracked, monotonic,
   empirically calibrated probability for odds comparison. This is the *only*
   genuinely ranking-neutral way to fix the displayed numbers - changing
   `LEAGUE.*PerPa` or `batterPaPrior` alters shrinkage per player and does reorder.
5. **Record offered American odds and whether a bet was placed.** Carried forward
   unaddressed from the 2026-07-17 report. Items 4 and 7 are only worth acting on
   if realized return can be measured; hit rate alone cannot establish
   profitability.

Shadow-test, do not ship:

6. **The four recentAdj arms** in Finding 6. Arm C (fix the shrinkage) is the one
   that tests the actual defect.
7. **Trailing-window league anchors** replacing the hardcoded constants
   (Finding 2). Addresses HR underconfidence, hit overconfidence, and the drift in
   both. Do not apply a static multiplier - the HR bias is -3.1 pp historically but
   -0.7 pp right now.
8. **`gameEnvFactor` removal** (Finding 3 ablation). Helps top 10, hurts top 5, so
   it conflicts with a five-pick consensus list. Belongs in the experiment, not in
   production.
9. **HR recency and contact** (Finding 5). Export the classic component factors
   alongside the current ones first.

Do not change:

10. **All HR scoring weights** (Finding 4), and the HR selection policy. The
    current-versus-classic HR comparison is one home run.
11. **`LEAGUE.hPerPa`** (Finding 3, retracted). It is correct at 0.223.

## Statistical Cautions

- Findings 2 and 3 rest on 8,556 rows over 38 slates and are the most secure here.
- Finding 1 rests on 99 picks over 8 slates. p ~ 0.055 on the headline comparison.
- Finding 4 is an argument *against* action from an underpowered sample, which is
  the direction underpowered samples should push.
- Finding 5 is unmeasured. It is a code observation plus a suggestive appearance
  count, not a result.
- Outcomes cluster by game and slate, and players repeat across dates, so row-level
  tests overstate certainty throughout.
- Audit rows are a backtest approximation that skips recent-form refinements and
  historical handedness splits, so audit and saved-pick populations are not
  strictly comparable.
- The single-trip rate reconstruction used in the ablations carries ~0.007 (hit)
  and ~0.0025 (HR) mean absolute error from three-decimal export rounding.
